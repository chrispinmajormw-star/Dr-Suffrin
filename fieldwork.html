TB case finding

When finding TB isn’t the same as finding TB cases

A person with a cough in a village is not yet a TB case. They become one only after a long chain of events goes right: someone notices, a sample is collected, it travels, it is tested, a result comes back, and treatment starts. Every link in that chain is a place where the person can quietly drop out of the system.

Programs are usually judged by the last number in the chain. The work happens in the links.

02

AI-enabled diagnostics

What happens when an AI X-ray finds an abnormality?

The software works in seconds. The image is flagged, a score appears, and the screen says the patient needs further testing. For the technology, the job is done. For the patient and the health system, it has just begun.

What happens when…

The patient lives far awayReturning for sputum or results costs a day’s income and a transport fare.
Transport is unavailableSamples and patients wait for a vehicle that serves many sites.
Electricity failsThe X-ray unit, the AI software and the GeneXpert machine all stop.
The diagnostic site is distantThe sample travels for days and results arrive after the patient has given up.
The patient doesn’t returnThe AI found the abnormality, and the system lost the person.

This is implementation science in its most practical form: a tool’s accuracy matters less than the pathway it sits in.

Health equity

The last mile of health care

A service can be technically available and still out of reach. The clinic is open, the drugs are in stock, the test exists. Yet for the person who needs it, the distance between home and that service is made of many things at once.

GeographyTransportPovertyCommunity healthFacility capacitySocial support

None of these is solved by a single intervention. A free test still costs a transport fare. A community health worker can bring care closer, but only if the facility behind them has capacity. An adolescent can be offered treatment, but adherence depends on school, food and family.

Closing the last mile means designing for the patient’s whole journey, not only for the point where the service is delivered.
